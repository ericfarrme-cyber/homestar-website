=== 2026-08-14 (later) — WHY OUR CONTENT IS INVISIBLE TO AI CRAWLERS ===
Triggered by Eric asking to get the new Zionsville kitchen project indexed "for AI and on Google".
Checking that surfaced a structural problem bigger than the one page.

WHAT WAS VERIFIED (not inferred):
1. robots.txt is wide open - "User-agent: * / Allow: /". The wildcard permits GPTBot, ClaudeBot,
   PerplexityBot, CCBot, Google-Extended and Bingbot. Crawler permission is NOT the blocker.
2. The raw HTML served for /projects/zionsville-kitchen-main-level is 3,732 bytes and contains
   ZERO of the project prose. `curl | grep "botanical mural"` returns 0 matches. Body is literally
   <div id="root"></div>. All content is painted client-side by React.
3. What a non-rendering crawler actually receives on that route:
     canonical ............. CORRECT, per-route  (the Aug 5 fix works)
     og:url ................ CORRECT, per-route
     <title> ............... GENERIC homepage title
     meta description ...... GENERIC homepage description
     og:title / og:desc .... GENERIC homepage copy
     body content .......... NONE

WHY IT IS LIKE THAT: scripts/build-route-heads.mjs, lines 26-30, deliberately declines to inject
per-route title/description, reasoning that "the app sets those at runtime and Google demonstrably
renders them". That reasoning is CORRECT FOR GOOGLE - Googlebot renders JavaScript. It was written
about Google and it does not carry over to AI crawlers, which mostly do not execute JS. The prior
decision was not wrong; it was scoped to a constraint that has since changed.

CALIBRATION - do not overstate this:
 - Bingbot DOES render JS, so the Bing-backed layer of ChatGPT search can still reach our content.
 - ChatGPT did cite thehomestarservice.com in one of the 12 protocol runs on 2026-08-14.
 So this is a serious structural handicap, NOT a total blackout. What IS unambiguous, regardless of
 rendering, is that every one of our 236 routes presents the same generic homepage title and
 description to any crawler, scraper, dataset builder or social unfurl that reads raw HTML.

WHY IT PLAUSIBLY EXPLAINS THINGS WE ALREADY MEASURED (hypothesis, worth testing):
 - The design-build claim shipped to the site has not been absorbed by the corpus (task #4).
 - ChatGPT describes HomeStar via BBB, Angi, Houzz, Reddit and HomeAdvisor - third parties - and
   rarely via our own copy.
 - The Aug 5 "moat evidence" quotes all traced to the REVIEW corpus, never to site prose.
 A site that yields nothing to a non-rendering fetch would produce exactly this pattern.
