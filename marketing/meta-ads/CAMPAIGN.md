# HomeStar — Meta / Facebook Ads

Creative built **2026-08-26**. 9 still concepts × 3 placements = 27 images, plus 2 video ads cut from the project walkthroughs. All in `renders/`.

**Whole-home is the focal concept** — three still variants (01 / 01b / 01c) plus the V1 video.

Every claim below is traceable to `src/App.jsx`. Nothing is invented — for a
licensed contractor, an unsupportable ad claim is a licensing problem, not just
a marketing one.

---

## The one rule for this account

**The photograph is never AI-generated, AI-edited, or AI-"enhanced" into an ad.**

You are selling completed work to people who will stand in that room. The photo
*is* the proof. We tested both Higgsfield image models on
`westfield-basement-masterpiece-1.jpg` and both redrew the room — see
`higgsfield-tests/` and the "Where Higgsfield fits" section below.

`build_ads.py` therefore only crops, scales and composites. The pixels of the
room are the pixels the photographer shot.

### Why 06 was rewritten

The first version led with *"Two friends of 20 years. One phone number."* That
reads well **if you already know who HomeStar is** — and on a cold Meta
audience nobody does. It never said what the company does, where it works, or
why the crew is different, so it asked for trust before earning it.

The rewrite puts the company first and keeps the warmth: *"HomeStar is two
friends from Fishers with a licensed crew of their own"* — name, place,
differentiator — with the services and service area in the subline.

## Suggested account structure

- **Objective:** Leads. Not Traffic — traffic optimises for cheap clicks, and
  cheap clicks on a $60K purchase are the wrong clicks.
- **Conversion event:** estimate-form submit. Nothing upstream of it.
- **Audience:** 8–12 mi radius on Carmel / Fishers / Westfield / Zionsville /
  Noblesville / Geist. Age 33+. Homeowners.
  Let Advantage+ expand once you have ~30 conversions/week.
- **Do not** add interest stacks on top of a radius this tight — you'll starve
  delivery.
- **Budget split to start:** whole-home is the focal, so put 45% of spend on the
  01 / 01b / 01c / V1 set in one ad set and let Meta pick the winner. Give 02
  (major projects, master craftsmanship) and 06 (who we are) 35% between them as the trust plays,
  and split the rest across 03/04/05/07.
- **Lead with V1.** Video is the cheapest reach on Reels and it is the only
  creative that proves multi-room scope by showing it.
- **Placements:** feed 4:5 and Reels 9:16 are the volume drivers. 1:1 is for
  carousels and the right column.

## Video ads

Two Reels/Stories cuts, both from footage already shot for these projects. The
sources are natively 1080×1920, so nothing is reframed, upscaled or generated —
same rule as the stills.

| File | Length | Cut from |
|------|--------|----------|
| `V1-whole-home-three-baths--reels-video.mp4` | ~22s | `geist-three-bath-video.mp4` |
| `V2-entertaining-floor--reels-video.mp4` | ~22s | `westfield-basement-masterpiece-video.mov` |

Each is: 3s hook → footage with a persistent wordmark and rating badge → 3.4s
brand end card with the CTA.

**Both ship silent, deliberately.**

- The Geist source carries a loud music bed (mean −11.3 dB, peaks at −0.6 dB)
  of unknown licence. A commercial track in a paid ad is a rights problem and
  Meta may mute or reject it.
- The Westfield source is silent already (−91 dB).

Add a track from **Meta's own royalty-free library inside Ads Manager** — it is
cleared for paid placements. Most Reels are watched muted anyway, which is why
the hook and end card carry the message on screen.

Two footage choices worth knowing: the V1 cut deliberately moves between three
visibly different bathrooms, because an earlier version spent 12 seconds in one
room and quietly broke the "three bathrooms" claim. The V2 cut avoids the gym
and the fireplace wall — both read as near-black on a phone in daylight.

## Compliance notes

- Meta's Housing special-ad-category applies to housing *sale and rental*
  advertising, not remodeling services. These should run as normal ads — but if
  the account has previously been flagged, expect targeting restrictions and
  keep the radius wide rather than fighting it.
- Every dollar figure shown is a published range from the site's own cost
  guides. If site pricing changes, these creatives must be re-rendered.

## Where Higgsfield fits

Tested on this account, on real project photos:

**❌ Not for the hero photograph.** Both `nano_banana_pro` and
`marketing_studio_image` were given the Westfield basement photo with explicit
"do not alter the photograph" instructions. Both redrew it:

- The framed poster (really *The Book of Mormon*) came back as
  "HBKN / TAB AOFT CBVGJUS OF THE CUBTGIG" and "MBRM.N / THE BEST MUGICAL OF THIS CONTURY"
- Bottle labels on the bar shelf became invented gibberish
- "STIGA" on the ping-pong table became "/TIGA"
- `marketing_studio_image` also duplicated words in the headline:
  "YOUR BASEMENT IS 1,200 SQ FT **SQ FT** YOU ALREADY OWN"

Side-by-side files are in `higgsfield-tests/`. For a licensed contractor
advertising completed work, that's a misrepresentation risk, not a style choice.

**✅ Worth using for:**
1. **Upscaling** low-res project photos — verified, and now in production.
   `westfield-basement-masterpiece-2.jpg` (1080×720, too small for a 4:5 feed
   ad) went through `upscale_image` to 4096×2737. Side-by-side in
   `higgsfield-tests/_upscale-compare.jpg`: room geometry, fixtures, flag and
   window are unchanged, wood grain genuinely resolves. That upscale is saved
   to `assets/` and is what concept 07 renders from.
2. **Motion.** Turning a still project photo into a 3–5s push-in for Reels.
   Reels is where the cheap reach is, and a static image underperforms there.
   Verify each clip frame-by-frame before it runs.
3. **Net-new brand graphics** that aren't a claim about a real project.

## Rendering

```bash
python marketing/meta-ads/build_ads.py
```

Filter to one concept: `python build_ads.py founders`.

To add a variant, append a dict to `ADS` in `build_ads.py`. Layouts are `band`
(solid navy block — maximum legibility) and `scrim` (gradient over photo —
more editorial). Reels always uses the card layout regardless of `layout`,
because hard-cropping a landscape interior to 9:16 destroys the room.

`fx`/`fy` (0–1) set the crop focal point when a photo needs to sit off-centre.

## Known constraints

- The photo library is mixed: the Zionsville, Geist three-bath and Westfield
  Masterpiece sets are professionally shot; much of the rest is jobsite phone
  documentation. Only the pro-shot subset is used here.
- `westfield-basement-masterpiece-*` is your strongest basement content but is
  1080×720. Shot 2 has been upscaled into `assets/`; the rest still need it
  before they can be used in feed placements.
- Reels layouts are checked against Meta's 1500px safe-zone limit on every
  render; the build prints the slack per concept and warns on overrun.
- The cartoon star mascot is deliberately not used. It reads playful-handyman
  and undercuts a $150K basement. The wordmark is used instead.

## Correction, 2026-09-06 - the in-house trades claim

Ads 01, 02, 05, 06 and the V1 video all asserted, in one form or another, that
HomeStar's plumbers and electricians are **on its own payroll** rather than
subcontracted. That has been removed everywhere.

**Eric's reasoning, and it is the right call:** the claim pigeonholes the
business into using its own people on every job. It removes the freedom to
bring in a specialist sub when a specialist is what the work deserves - which
on a premium job is often exactly what you want.

**What replaces it:** the claim is now about the standard and the
accountability, both of which are true whoever is holding the tools.

- All plumbing by licensed plumbers, all electrical by licensed electricians.
- One schedule across every trade, and one contractor answerable for all of it.
- On specialist work, the trade is chosen to fit the job rather than by who
  happens to be free.

**Ad 02 was rewritten twice.** The first replacement still argued about who
holds the tools, just more carefully - accountability rather than payroll.
Eric's call was to leave that territory entirely and build the ad on the
existing slogan, **Major Projects. Master Craftsmanship.**

That is the better ad, and not only because it sidesteps the problem. The
staffing argument asks a homeowner to care about an org chart. The craft
argument shows them a 14-foot mantle, a mosaic centred on its drain and
hand-glazed tile where no two pieces match - four things a competitor cannot
say because they have not done them. It is evidence rather than assertion, and
every item in it traces to a real job in the portfolio.

**Also swept:** two reel captions, four places on the website, and the
`REELS.md` record. One live organic post from 27 August still carries the old
claim and needs editing on Facebook - it is an ordinary Page post, so editing
preserves its likes and comments.
