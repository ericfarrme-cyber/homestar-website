## What the room is

a near-black textured accent wall behind the vanity, shot through with copper and rust.

## Problems found

1. **Clip 194308_772 shows the videographer's face.** A man in sunglasses is clearly
   recognisable in the mirror from about 7.5s to the end. Confirmed at full resolution.
   The clip is only usable 0–7s, and 194259_148 covers the same subject cleanly for 10.7s.
   Use that instead.
2. **Heavy duplicate coverage.** Six pairs of near-identical clips: 194559/194603/194611,
   194628/194633, 194642/194647, 194654/194659, 194450/194509, 194259/194308. The shoot has
   29 clips but roughly **12 distinct subjects.** Build the cut from subjects, not from clips —
   that is what caused four rounds of rework on the Adams reel.
3. **The window blows out** in most of the vanity-wall clips facing it. Usable, but anything
   holding on the window needs grading or needs to be cut around.
4. **Before frames are low resolution** (~1 MP phone screenshots). Reel-safe, website-marginal.
5. **The sconces in the room are not the sconces in the bid.** The bid lists two 3-light
   brushed-nickel fixtures; the room has three vertical linear bars. The bid predates the
   change. Do not publish a lighting spec from it.

## Corrections the paperwork forced

Three things that would have gone out wrong if the Virginia Tile **order** had not been read
after the **selection sheet**:

1. **The shower walls are not Travertine Silver.** The selection sheet specifies it; the order
   acknowledgement never buys it. One line, 256 SF of Travertine Roman, covers floor and shower
   wall together — and the photographs agree, the two surfaces are the same tile.
2. **The sconce count.** The bid says two 3-light fixtures. A change order replaced them with
   four sconces, which is what is on the wall.
3. **The accent wall is wallpaper**, not a slab or a panel — and it runs to the ceiling at about
   64 sq ft. That was the single most-guessed detail in the first pass of this review.

A selection sheet is what somebody chose. An order acknowledgement is what arrived. Only the
second one is a fact.

## Correction, 2026-11-14

The scope of work says "Schluter-Kerdi waterproofing membrane on all shower walls". The
build photos say otherwise: R06 and R07 show **Kerdi-Board** panels with the Schluter
branding and the ANSI A118.10 marking still legible, washered fasteners, and banded seams.
Board and membrane are different products — the board is substrate and waterproofing in
one, the membrane is a sheet bonded over a separate substrate — so this matters when the
copy names one.

Corrected on the case study and in the Kerdi blog post, both of which had followed the
scope. Same lesson as the tile: the paperwork is what was specified, the photographs are
what was built.
